FAIR Nursing Study

Building fairer research environments for Black nursing academics.

Explore evidence informed, co-designed recommendations that place responsibility for change with organisations and sector bodies.

Recommendations available

About the study

FAIR examines the barriers Black nursing academics face when developing research careers in non Russell Group universities in England. Interviews informed co-design workshops with Black academics from health and social care disciplines, institutional leads and research support staff.

The recommendations below were refined with Project Steering Committee feedback. They address organisational responsibility, national relevance and intersecting forms of disadvantage.

Who should use these recommendations?

University leaders, research offices, funders, regulators, professional bodies and partners shaping research careers.

Recommendation 01

Protected research time

Organisations should ring fence research time supported by workload health checks and transparent appraisal objectives aligned with research expectations.

Why this matters and how to apply it
Why it matters
Sustained research engagement requires formal protection rather than reliance on goodwill. This addresses structural inequities that limit Black nursing academics’ access to time and resources.
Wider system
Funders and regulators can incentivise equitable workload models through funding criteria, accreditation and sector benchmarking.
Intersectional considerations
Monitor impacts across ethnicity, gender, disability, caring responsibilities, contract type and career stage.

Recommendation 02

Anti racism charter

Institutions should co-produce an anti racism charter that incorporates facilitated safe dialogue spaces, transparent public reporting, and clear alignment between the charter’s commitments and institutional research strategies.

Why this matters and how to apply it
Why it matters
Shifts responsibility from individuals to institutions and legitimises discussion of structural and institutional racism within academia.
Wider system
Align with national frameworks, such as the Advance HE Race Equality Charter, and engage funders, regulators and professional bodies to reinforce sector wide accountability.
Intersectional considerations
Recognise compounded disadvantage, including gendered racism, disability and migration status.
Link to this recommendation
Recommendation 03

Structured dialogue and cultural taxation

Institutions should establish facilitated forums for discussing racism and formally recognise cultural taxation within workload and promotion systems.

Why this matters and how to apply it
Why it matters
Reduces invisible labour, challenges institutional silence around racism, and supports retention, wellbeing and research capacity.
Wider system
Share learning with sector bodies to shape expectations for inclusive research cultures and staff support standards.
Intersectional considerations
Acknowledge overlapping marginalised identities and their different effects.
Link to this recommendation
Recommendation 04

Mentorship and sponsorship

Institutions should develop formal mentoring and sponsorship schemes that support Black nursing academics’ progression. Mentors and sponsors should be recognised in promotion criteria and workload models. Opportunities for reciprocal or reverse mentoring should be properly resourced.

Why this matters and how to apply it
Why it matters
Strengthens progression, reduces isolation and enhances research visibility and quality.
Wider system
Collaboration between institutions can expand mentoring pools and support national capacity building.
Intersectional considerations
Build intersectional awareness into mentor training and matching to address compounded barriers.
Link to this recommendation
Recommendation 05

Equity governance

Institutions should establish an Equity Board with delegated authority, senior leadership involvement and transparent reporting mechanisms.

Why this matters and how to apply it
Why it matters
Sustained change requires structural oversight, decision making authority and accountability at senior levels.
Wider system
Engage funders, regulators, NHS partners and professional bodies to underpin wider accountability and shared standards.
Intersectional considerations
Ensure representation reflects diversity across intersecting identities, roles and levels of seniority.
Link to this recommendation
Recommendation 06

Leadership accountability

Institutions should embed anti racism and equity indicators within leadership appraisal and performance review processes.

Why this matters and how to apply it
Why it matters
Links equity commitments to accountability, reward and institutional priorities.
Wider system
Align with national charters and sector benchmarks to strengthen legitimacy and comparability.
Intersectional considerations
Measure intersecting disparities rather than single characteristics in isolation, and report trends over time.
Link to this recommendation
Recommendation 07

Equity audits

Organisations should conduct regular, transparent audits of workload, pay, funding success and career progression, combining quantitative data with evidence from lived experience.

Why this matters and how to apply it
Why it matters
Makes hidden inequities visible and supports targeted, evidence informed action.
Wider system
Share findings with sector bodies and contribute to national datasets and benchmarks.
Intersectional considerations
Analyse intersecting patterns so aggregated categories do not conceal disparities.
Link to this recommendation
Recommendation 08

Cross sector research partnerships

Institutions, funders and sector partners should build collaborative research forums with the NHS, charities and professional bodies, including shared development, funding and dissemination pathways.

Why this matters and how to apply it
Why it matters
Expands opportunities, reduces institutional silos and broadens access to funding, mentorship and dissemination.
Wider system
Supports coordinated national efforts to grow inclusive research capacity and equitable access to resources.
Intersectional considerations
Ensure diverse representation across intersecting identities in partnership governance and activities.
Link to this recommendation
Recommendation 09

Leadership pipeline and representation

Institutions should set transparent targets for senior representation and establish leadership development cohorts supported by sponsorship.

Why this matters and how to apply it
Why it matters
Visible leadership influences culture, curricula and workforce aspirations while sponsorship accelerates equitable progression.
Wider system
Collaborate with national bodies to support sustainability and sector alignment.
Intersectional considerations
Targets should reflect intersecting representation, such as Black women professors, rather than aggregated categories.
Link to this recommendation

Using the recommendations

Start with institutional accountability

Review decisions about workloads, progression and resources alongside the experiences of those affected. Assign responsibility at a level with authority to act.

Keep the original meaning

These actions are directed at institutions, funders, regulators and sector bodies. Implementation should not create further unpaid work for Black academics.
